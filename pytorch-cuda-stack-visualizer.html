03 — The compiler assembly line

What torch.compile actually does

The PyTorch compiler stack is a pipeline of specialists: TorchDynamo captures your code, AOT Autograd traces the backward pass ahead of time, and a backend — usually TorchInductor driving OpenAI’s Triton — writes new, fused GPU kernels for your exact hardware.

TorchDynamo

GRAPH CAPTURE

Watches your Python at the bytecode level and captures what the model really does into an FX graph. Regular Python control flow (if, loops) just works — no fragile tracing.

AOT Autograd

AHEAD-OF-TIME DIFFERENTIATION

Traces the backward pass before training runs, so forward + backward become one joint graph the backend can optimize as a whole.

TorchInductor

DEFAULT BACKEND · CODEGEN

The most common backend: it generates code — Triton kernels for GPUs (C++/OpenMP for CPUs). Swap in the XLA backend instead for TPUs and other devices.

Triton (OpenAI)

FUSION + AUTOTUNING

Takes dozens of small ops and fuses them into one kernel, then benchmark-tunes its config for your specific GPU & shapes. Deep dive in Chapter 14.

05 — Why it all matters

The goal: GPUs doing useful work, not waiting

An idle GPU burns the same money as a busy one. System-level tuning shrinks the red gaps — the GPU waiting on the CPU, on memory/disk I/O, or on other GPUs during synchronization. Hover the red segments to see each cause.

System-level tuning is often overlooked in favor of model optimizations — yet small OS-level tweaks can yield double-digit percentage gains. At the scale of a big AI project, that’s tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars in saved compute.
mini-glossary

Words the book will keep using

kernel — a function that runs on the GPU itself.
GEMM — general matrix multiply; the workhorse of deep learning.
cuBLAS / cuDNN / NCCL — NVIDIA’s optimized libraries for matmuls / convolutions / multi-GPU communication.
kernel fusion — merging many small kernels into one, avoiding memory round-trips.
autotuning — benchmarking kernel variants on your exact GPU to pick the fastest.
HBM — the GPU’s high-bandwidth memory; fast, but slower than on-chip registers.
SM — streaming multiprocessor; one of the GPU’s thousands of core blocks.
eager vs compiled — run each op immediately vs. capture a graph and optimize it first.
